## Experiment 3: gradient boosting

**Question:** Can sequential shallow trees improve on the existing forest while holding features and preprocessing fixed?

Use the existing numeric passenger attributes plus FamilySize, IsAlone, and grouped Title. Numeric columns receive training medians and scaling; categorical columns receive training modes and one-hot encoding. PassengerId and raw Name, Cabin, and Ticket are excluded from model inputs.

Fixed settings: 200 boosting stages, learning rate 0.05, random state 42. Search four configurations, ranked only by development CV accuracy:

| Maximum depth | Minimum samples per leaf | Mean CV accuracy | Fold standard deviation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 5 | 0.8062 | 0.0113 |
| 2 | 10 | 0.8118 | 0.0194 |
| 3 | 5 | 0.8216 | 0.0267 |
| 3 | 10 | **0.8287** | 0.0210 |

**Conclusion:** the selected configuration is 0.42 percentage points below the forest's mean CV accuracy and also lower on the reused holdout. This experiment does not justify replacing random forest as the current CV leader. The Kaggle upload scored **0.76315**, which is 1.436 percentage points below the historical forest score of 0.77751. The result supports retaining the forest as our leading approach. This comparison includes differences between the historical and cleaned preprocessing; it does not isolate the model algorithm alone.
